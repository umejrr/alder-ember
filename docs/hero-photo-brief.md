# Homepage hero photograph: shoot brief

For Alder & Ember and the photographer. It describes the one image the homepage opens with, what the website needs from it, and how to deliver it. Everything below is written to match how the site actually lays the image out, so the finished photograph can go straight in.

The photograph on the site today is a **real photograph supplied by the owner and approved as the hero on 20 October 2026**: a dark timber cabin with a glazed front, lit at dusk, with a hedge and silver birch behind. It is the look benchmark for every other image on the site (see [photo-brief.md](photo-brief.md)). It is 1884 × 835 px, which is too small for very large screens, and its photographer, licence terms and which sauna it shows are still to be recorded. This brief remains the specification for a higher-resolution replacement or a vertical companion frame.

## 1. The picture in one paragraph

A single, still, wide photograph of **one real Alder & Ember sauna in a real UK garden at dusk**, lit warmly from inside, standing on its base on a lawn with soft planting behind it. The cabin is the clear subject, large enough to read as a product, set slightly right of centre. The left of the frame is calm and dark so the headline can sit there. It should feel quiet, grounded and architectural: a considered object in a garden, not a lifestyle advert. No people, no props that distract, no theatrical effects.

The emotional promise is the site's line: **"A quieter moment, just outside."**

## 2. Subject

- **Which sauna.** One model, shot exactly as it is sold. Our recommendation is **the Alder**, because it has the glazed front, which gives the warm interior glow that makes this image work, and it is the middle of the range. The Rowan and Ember are shot separately (see the main brief). Whichever model is used, the photograph must show the **standard product**: the real cladding, real glazing, real door and hardware, the real heater flue if it has one. No one-off custom additions and no options we do not sell. This image becomes product evidence, so it must be accurate.
- **Condition.** Brand new and clean: no scuffs, no weathering, no stickers, nothing left on the roof.
- **Door.** Closed, with the glazed front lit from inside so the interior (benches, warm timber) is visible through the glass. If the model has a solid door, light the interior through the glazing or window only.
- **Base.** A simple, honest base: compacted gravel, paving or a plain timber platform, whichever the product really needs. Do not show decking, steps, planters or an electrical connection that would suggest they are included in the price. (The site says ground preparation and electrical connection are assessed separately.)
- **Steam and smoke.** None added. Only a natural flue plume, and only if the shot is of a wood-burning configuration and it is really happening.

## 3. Setting

- **A believable UK garden**, not a mountain lake or a Mediterranean terrace: lawn, a timber fence or mature hedge, informal planting. Native trees are ideal. **Silver birch or alder** behind the cabin would be a quiet nod to the name.
- Naturalistic and uncluttered. Avoid hot tubs, pools, patio sets, bright cushions, string lights, garden ornaments, and anything plastic or brightly coloured.
- No visible neighbours, house numbers, number plates, signage or third-party brands. A distant, soft roofline of a house is fine if it helps the garden feel real.
- It should feel like a place someone lives. It can be a customer's garden (with a signed property release) or a showroom or yard garden. Say which, because it affects how the image may be labelled on the site (section 9).

## 4. Light and mood

- **Time.** Shoot in the **balanced twilight window, roughly 15 to 40 minutes after sunset**, when the sky still has deep blue colour and the lit interior is about as bright as the sky. That gives the glow without blown highlights, and it makes the foreground naturally dark, which the headline needs.
- **Back-up sets** from the same position: soft golden-hour side light, and a quiet overcast daytime frame. Daytime works only if the lower left of the frame is shaded (hedge, trees), otherwise the white headline will not be legible without a heavy darkening wash that would spoil the photograph.
- **Look.** Natural and restrained. Neutral white balance, true timber colour, a warm glow from the interior only. **No heavy orange grade, no HDR look, no crushed blacks, no fake atmosphere.** Shadows keep some detail; the cabin must read clearly.
- **Palette** to keep in mind (the site's): warm canvas `#f4f0e8`, deep forest `#111c16`, timber `#795b45`, and a very small ember accent `#a54f32`. Greens, natural timber, charcoal and the interior glow are all on-palette; saturated colours are not.

## 5. Composition (how the website uses the frame)

The homepage shows this photograph full-bleed behind the header, with the headline, short paragraph and two buttons over its lower left. So the frame has to leave room for them. The diagram is the **16:9 desktop crop**.

```
 0%                                                             100%
+------------------------------------------------------------------+ 0%
|  header zone: calm dark sky or foliage, no bright detail         |
|  (logo left, navigation right, light text)                       | 12%
|                                                                  |
|            sky / trees                    roof, flue             |
|                                      +----------------+          |
|                                      |                |          |
|   QUIET ZONE for headline            |   SAUNA        |          |
|   left 45% of the width,             |   door and     |          |
|   from 45% to 90% down:              |   glazed front |          |
|   dark lawn, hedge or shadow.        |   centred at   |          |
|   No door, no bright object,         |   60 to 70%    |          |
|   no heater, no face.                |   across       |          |
|                                      +----------------+          |
|   dark, low-detail foreground (lawn, gravel)                     | 90%
+------------------------------------------------------------------+ 100%
```

- **Sauna size and position.** The cabin spans about **45% to 90% of the width** and from about **22% to 85% of the height**. Its **door and glazing sit between 55% and 75% across**, never under the headline, which ends at about 45% of the width.
- **Quiet zone.** The left 45% of the width, from 45% to 90% down, is dark and low in detail: lawn, hedge, tree trunks or shadow. No door, heater, bright lamp or person there.
- **Header zone.** The top 12% to 15% is calm and fairly dark, because the navigation sits over it in light text. Keep the top of the roof and any flue below this line.
- **Ground.** The bottom 15% to 20% is darker, low-detail foreground. It is where the buttons sit, and on phones it fades into the deep forest panel under the image.
- **Bottom right** has a small caption on preview builds. Keep key detail out of the last 8% there.
- **Verticals stay vertical.** No converging walls and no tilted horizon.

### The two frames to shoot from the same position

1. **Horizontal master, 3:2, native.** The site crops this to 16:9 (1280 and 1920 px wide). Cropping 3:2 to 16:9 loses about 16% of the height, so **treat the top and bottom 8% of the 3:2 frame as expendable** and put nothing essential there.
2. **A separate vertical frame, 4:5 or 2:3 portrait.** On phones the image sits on top of the text on a dark panel, about 390 × 480 px (4:5). This is a different composition, not a crop of the wide shot: the **whole cabin, roof to base, centred**, with the **top 25% quiet** (the header and menu button sit there) and the **bottom 35% dark and low-detail** so it can fade into the forest-green panel. If a 2:3 vertical is shot, the top and bottom 8% are expendable.

### Social share image

While the camera is in position, also capture a **text-free 1200 × 630 composition** (about 1.9:1): sauna centred, everything important within the middle 80%. The site emits no social-share image until an approved one of Alder & Ember's own work exists, and this is the cheapest way to get it.

## 6. Camera

- **Body and files.** Full-frame or good APS-C, **RAW**, 24 MP or more, on a **tripod** (twilight exposures are slow).
- **Height and lens.** Camera at **about 1.2 to 1.5 m** (chest to eye height), so scale feels human. **A 28 to 35 mm equivalent lens** is ideal; 24 mm only if the garden is tight. No ultra-wide distortion. A tilt-shift lens is ideal, otherwise shoot slightly wider and correct verticals in post.
- **Settings.** f/8 to f/11 for depth, lowest practical ISO. Bracket 3 to 5 exposures and blend naturally, or shoot at the balanced moment so one frame holds.
- **Focus.** On the glazed front or door, with the whole cabin sharp.
- **Variation.** 5 to 8 final candidates: different times of the window, a slightly different distance, and a slightly different angle. We will pick against the layout.

## 7. What to avoid (summary)

People or faces; competitor or third-party products and logos; added steam, fire or smoke; heavy orange grading; HDR; dramatic storm or cinematic skies; clutter or styling props beyond two or three quiet ones (a folded natural-linen towel on a timber stool, a plain timber bucket); anything implying included extras; a bright foreground or a bright object under the headline.

## 8. The other pictures the site needs

The main brief lists every other photograph the site needs (17 in all, with sizes, crops and direction for each). Shooting them in the same session and the same light is what makes the site feel like one place.

## 9. Delivery and rights

- **Files.** The RAW files for the selected frames, plus graded **16-bit TIFFs or full-quality JPEGs in sRGB**. The long edge should be **at least 6000 px** for the hero. The site serves up to 1920 px wide, so 3840 px covers high-density screens.
- **Licence.** A written licence covering **website and marketing use** with no credit requirement (or the exact credit line, if there is one), and a **property release** from the garden's owner.
- **Privacy.** No identifiable people, house numbers, number plates or neighbours.
- **A record for each image** (the site tracks this for every photograph): which model and configuration is pictured, date and location, photographer and licence, **whether it is product evidence** (a real Alder & Ember sauna, exactly as sold) **or atmosphere**, and whether it shows a **real customer installation**. A showroom or yard garden is product evidence but is **not** a customer project and will not be labelled as one.
- **Suggested alt text** (to confirm against the final frame): *"The Alder, a timber garden sauna with a glazed front, glowing at dusk on a lawn edged with birch trees."*

## 10. What happens after delivery

1. The chosen frames are cropped for each use and prepared in several file sizes, with a separate square crop for phones.
2. The headline and paragraph are measured against the real photograph at six screen widths. If the headline falls under 3:1 contrast (or the paragraph under 4.5:1), the darkening behind the text or the crop is adjusted, or a different candidate is chosen.
3. The photograph is marked approved with its rights and a written description, which removes any preview label.
4. The approved 1200 × 630 frame becomes the link-preview image.
